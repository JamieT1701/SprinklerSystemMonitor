An easy, picture-first walkthrough for putting your ESP32, ADS1115, pressure circuit and temperature sensor connections onto two small 50 × 70 mm perfboards.

🔩 2 perfboards you already own🌡️ 4.7 kΩ temperature pull-up added⚡ 4.7 kΩ pressure lower resistor🛠️ No custom PCB
Important changes to the old guide: The DS18B20 probe has no built-in 4.7 kΩ resistor. We must install one on Board B. We're also replacing the previous 5 kΩ pressure resistor with your second 4.7 kΩ resistor. The firmware still needs its divider factor updated before final pressure calibration.
This is a supplemental monitor, not a sprinkler safety device. Unplug PoE before changing wires. Leave valves, piping, listed controls, fire alarm supervision, heat and inspections unchanged. Concept artwork below is for placement only: follow the schematic and pin labels for wiring.

2

Bring electronics back to the bench

  1. Unplug the Ethernet cable that supplies PoE. Disconnect USB too, if connected.
  2. Take a few photos so you know where the old wires went.
  3. Label the red, black and green pressure wires and the three temperature-probe wires.
  4. Unplug only the monitor electronics. Leave the installed pressure transducer on the compressor piping.
  5. Bring the ESP32/PoE board and ADS module back to your bench.
The monitor is offline while you build. Existing required fire protection and inspection arrangements must remain in place.
3

Lay the boards out (no drilling yet)

Mount the long ESP32 board separately; it's about 73 mm long and doesn't properly fit on a 70 mm perfboard. Put Board A and Board B beside it on a plastic baseplate.

Illustration is a placement idea, not a drilling template or an exact picture of your boards.

Before soldering, put the real parts down, check RJ45/USB access and decide how you'll get a screwdriver into the terminals.

4

Put sensor terminals on Board B

Board B will have two 3-screw terminal blocks. Put a clear label beside each screw before wiring anything.

J1 — PRESSURE

1 · +5V 2 · GND 3 · SIG

Later, connect the transducer's red, black, green wires in that order.

J2 — TEMPERATURE

1 · 3V3 2 · DATA 3 · GND

Later, connect the probe's 3V3, DATA and GND wires to the labeled screws.

These screw numbers are labels you assign. Before soldering, make sure they match the actual orientation of your terminal blocks, even when viewed upside down.
5

Add the missing temperature resistor

New correction: Your DS18B20 cable does not contain a 4.7 kΩ pull-up. We are adding it ourselves on Board B.

  1. Find a resistor marked 4.7 kΩ. This one is called R5.
  2. Solder one leg to the 3V3 wire / J2-1 connection.
  3. Solder the other leg to the DATA wire / J2-2 / ESP GPIO18 connection.
  4. Make sure the DATA wire still runs straight from ESP GPIO18 to J2-2. The resistor is a bridge between 3V3 and DATA, not a piece in the middle of the DATA wire.
  5. Connect J2-3 directly to GND.
Check: only one 4.7 kΩ pull-up is fitted to this temperature DATA line — the one you just added on Board B.

7

Solder the pressure resistors on Board A

We chose your second 4.7 kΩ resistor instead of buying a 5 kΩ. Label the resistors as you place them.

  1. Take R1 = 10 kΩ. One end goes to the incoming pressure SIG wire from Board B. The other end becomes the little point called DIV.
  2. Take R2 = 10 kΩ. Solder one end to DIV.
  3. Take your second 4.7 kΩ resistor. This is R3. Join it end-to-end with R2. Connect the far end of R3 to GND.
  4. Take R4 = 1 kΩ. Solder one end to DIV; the other end becomes the point called A0.
  5. Solder the 100 nF ceramic capacitor C1 between that A0 point and GND.
  6. Run a short insulated wire from A0 to the ADS pin labeled A0.
  7. Add small, well-spaced test loops labeled SIG and A0 so you can reach them with a meter.
Do not connect raw pressure SIG directly to ADS A0. ADS1115 runs at 3.3V; its ±4.096V measurement setting does not allow physical input above the supply rail. This divider still needs electrical verification.

9

Check everything BEFORE plugging in power

□ No solder bridge between 3V3 and GND
□ No solder bridge between 5V and GND
□ R5 really connects 3V3 ↔ DATA
□ R3 is 4.7 kΩ, not 5 kΩ
□ A0 not shorted to GND or 5V
□ No exposed metal touching the PoE assembly

Use your multimeter in continuity mode with power OFF. The lower pressure resistor branch legitimately connects toward ground through resistance; that is not the same as a direct zero-ohm short.

10

Bench test with a fake pressure signal

  1. Keep the actual pressure transducer disconnected from J1.
  2. Power the ESP32 from one supported power source. The ADS should appear at I2C 0x48. A0 near zero while J1 is empty can be expected.
  3. Power OFF. Take an extra temporary 10 kΩ resistor. Put one end on ESP 3V3, the other on Board B J1-3 SIG.
  4. Power on. With the new circuit, ADS A0 should read approximately 1.40V. Existing pressure firmware may display about 69 PSI. This is a fake test signal, not real pressure.
  5. Power OFF. Remove the temporary resistor completely.
What success means: Your divider and ADS analog signal path respond to a known test. It does not prove the warehouse transducer or actual pressure calibration.
11

Reinstall and compare with the real gauge

Mount the boards on insulating standoffs inside an enclosure roomy enough for a screwdriver, RJ45 bend, cable glands and test points. Reconnect sensors only while power is OFF.

Important firmware note

The pressure-v4 firmware currently installed used a 0.6000 divider. Your new resistors require 0.59514.

We'll update and recompile firmware before claiming calibrated PSI. Do not use the reading to trigger real fire-protection decisions.

We previously saw A0 near 0.0003V at a gauge pressure near 50 PSI. If that remains the case, first check the green sensor signal and 5V/common ground; don't unsolder the whole device.
