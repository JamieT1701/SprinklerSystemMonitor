01

Only three cables connect from outside

One Ethernet cable. One 3-wire pressure sensor. One 3-wire temperature probe. The two sensor cables use two 3-position terminal blocks. There is no third sensor block and no separate installed power connector.

ConnectionWhere it landsIts job
Ethernet / PoEExisting ESP32 RJ45 socketNetwork connection and power
Pressure cableJ1 on Board BThree wires: +5V, ground, signal
Temperature cableJ2 on Board BThree wires: 3V3, data, ground
The temperature sensor does not connect to ADS1115. It communicates directly with GPIO18. The ADS1115 is used only for pressure.
02

Use two small perfboards, not one giant board

Keep the existing controller and its factory PoE daughterboard together. Mount that assembly separately, then mount your two 50 x 70 mm perfboards beside it.

AssemblyWhat goes on it
ControllerExisting ESP32-S3 + PoE module; Ethernet stays here
Board AADS1115; R1, R2, R3, R4; C1 and C2. No sensor terminal blocks.
Board BExactly J1 and J2, the R5 temperature pull-up, and labeled solder pads for internal wires.

Use the confirmed-fitting P4 PETG mounting plate (about 95.2 mm wide x 142.7 mm tall), not the older suggested 170 x 120 mm baseplate. Its 3 mm perforated surface has four 6 mm integral feet. Those feet attach the PLATE to the plastic case; the ESP32 and both perfboards need their own insulated standoffs or ties. Arrange actual parts on P4 and check PoE underside, RJ45 cable bend, sensor-terminal screwdriver access, lid closure and rail clearance before securing anything. Do not drill the ESP board.

Use isolated-pad perfboard. Adjacent holes are not connected unless you deliberately solder a wire between them. Do not substitute continuous stripboard without redesigning its copper cuts.

04

Disconnect, label, and dry-fit

  1. Unplug PoE and any USB power. Photograph the existing electronics before moving wires.
  2. Label both sensor cables at the electronics end. Bring the electronic monitor to the bench; leave the pressure transducer screwed into its existing piping.
  3. Place the P4 plate in the case with its feet against the case floor. Do not drill the case yet if the actual screw/insert positions have not been verified. Insert each M3 heat-set fitting into a test socket first; actual insert dimensions may differ. The P4 print is PETG, not PLA.
  4. For final mounting, fit the brass inserts into the bottoms of the four printed feet using a suitable temperature-controlled heat-set tip, then screw from OUTSIDE the box into the inserts. M3 x 6 mm with flat washers is a tentative starting size, contingent on measured case wall, washer thickness and insert length. Do not bottom out a screw, overheat a foot, or crack the case.
  5. Lay out the controller, Board A and Board B on separate insulated supports over the P4 plate. Use perforations for mounting, keeping solder joints clear of the plate and hardware. Face the two sensor wire-entry openings toward an accessible edge.
  6. Label the two blocks J1 PRESSURE and J2 TEMP. Put labels on removable plugs too. Identical plugs can still be swapped even when individually keyed.
  7. Use the terminal numbering shown below when looking at the screw heads from above. Mark the actual pads before turning the board over. The underside is a mirror image.

Pressure colors come from your documented transducer wiring. Temperature cable colors can vary: preserve the already identified VCC, DATA and GND functions rather than assuming a generic color scheme.

05

Build Board B: the two sensor connections

  1. Solder the two terminal blocks to Board B with room for a screwdriver and wire strain relief.
  2. Choose and label five small solder-pad areas: +5V, +3V3, GND, DATA, SIG. They are not extra terminal blocks.
  3. Join J1-1 to the +5V pad. Join J1-3 to the SIG pad.
  4. Join J2-1 to the +3V3 pad. Join J2-2 to the DATA pad.
  5. Join J1-2 and J2-3 to the same GND pad.
  6. Solder R5, one 4.7k resistor, between the +3V3 pad and DATA pad. It bridges J2-1 and J2-2; it is not in series with DATA and does not go to ground.
  7. Leave the field sensor screws empty for now. Internal wires are soldered to the pads, not crowded under those screws.
Your probe has no built-in pull-up. R5 supplies the one required pull-up. Do not add another 4.7k on the same temperature DATA line.
06

Build Board A: pressure electronics

Mount the ADS1115 using sockets or insulated supports suitable for your actual breakout. Its printed pin labels, not a generic picture, determine where wires attach.

  1. Make labeled pad areas for SIG, DIV, MID, A0, GND and +3V3. Leave room for the actual ADS breakout.
  2. Solder R1 10k: one leg at SIG, the other at DIV.
  3. Solder R2 10k: one leg at DIV, the other at MID.
  4. Solder R3 4.7k: one leg at MID, the other at GND. R2 and R3 form the 14.7k lower leg in series.
  5. Solder R4 1k: one leg at DIV, the other at A0.
  6. Solder C1 100 nF: one leg at A0 and one at GND. Ceramic capacitors have no polarity.
  7. Run a short wire from the A0 pad to the ADS pin actually labeled A0. Connect ADS GND to the GND pad and ADS VDD to the +3V3 pad.
  8. Solder C2 100 nF across ADS VDD and GND, close to the breakout. This is supply filtering, not a second pressure divider.
  9. Keep ADS ADDR at GND for address 0x48. Preserve an existing ground strap; if ADDR is exposed and otherwise unconnected, connect it to ADS GND. Leave A1, A2, A3 and ALERT unused.
Raw sensor SIG must never bypass R1 and go straight to ADS A0. +5V never goes to ADS VDD. The divider is for this nominal 5V sensor; it is not a surge protector or a guarantee against miswiring.

08

Do the power-off checks on your bench

  1. Unplug both field sensors, Ethernet and USB. Inspect the solder side under a bright light for bridges and whiskers.
  2. Use continuity mode to check every intended ground connection and each W01-W09 end-to-end. Wiggle the wire gently while checking.
  3. Verify the +5V and +3V3 pad groups are not shorted together and neither is directly shorted to ground. Semiconductor paths and charging capacitors can affect meter readings; a beep alone is not a complete diagnosis.
  4. Verify R5 bridges J2-1 and J2-2, not J2-2 and J2-3. Check resistor values before connecting the controller when practical.
  5. Verify SIG reaches R1, A0 reaches the ADC A0 pin, and R2/R3 have a secure ground return. Leave the resistor joints strain-free.
  6. Make sure the baseplate and standoffs keep every solder joint clear of the controller and PoE daughterboard.
Never use continuity/resistance mode on a powered circuit. Keep all assembly work away from sprinkler valves, pressurized fittings and listed control wiring.
09

Test electronics before going back to the warehouse

A. Power and communication

  1. With both field sensors unplugged, power the controller from the existing working PoE connection. Use only one power source during these tests.
  2. At the accessible pads, verify approximately 3.3V on +3V3 and approximately 5V on +5V, relative to GND. ADS1115 should be detected at address 0x48.
  3. With pressure J1 empty, a near-zero A0 is expected because R1/R2/R3 pull that input toward ground. An unplugged temperature sensor must report a fault/unavailable state, not a real temperature.

B. Fake pressure signal - field sensor disconnected

  1. Power OFF. Connect a spare temporary 10k resistor from Board B +3V3 to J1-3 SIG. It is in addition to R1-R5.
  2. Power on. With a 3.30V source and the listed nominal values, A0 should be about 1.398V. Small resistor/supply tolerances change the exact value.
  3. The prepared v5-47k firmware calculates about 69.34 PSI from that fake signal. Older v4 calculates about 68.62 PSI. Neither number is actual pressure.
  4. Power OFF and remove the temporary resistor completely. Do not leave it connected when plugging in the real transducer.

C. Temperature

When the probe is available, connect its three wires to J2 while powered off. On power-up, confirm a plausible temperature and a gentle response to warming the probe by hand. Power off, disconnect it, then power on: the reading must become a fault/unavailable state. Reconnect only with power off.

The simulator verifies the divider/ADC path. It does not prove the pressure transducer works, and it must not be adjusted to display 50 PSI.
10

Reinstall, then compare with the real gauge

  1. Remove all test leads. With PoE disconnected, connect the actual pressure cable to J1 and the temperature cable to J2.
  2. Secure the boards and cable strain relief. Place the temperature probe in the riser room near vulnerable equipment, away from direct heater discharge and pipe contact.
  3. Restore PoE and compare the calculated pressure to the nearby mechanical gauge. Use the reading the gauge shows at that moment, not an assumed fixed 50 PSI.
  4. If the real sensor still produces near-zero A0 after the simulator passed, investigate the transducer supply, return and signal cable. Rebuilding the perfboard does not by itself establish that the sensor is fixed.

The 0-150 PSI, 0.5-4.5V sensor specification is supported by the older project records but its actual make/model is still unverified. A single roughly 50 PSI comparison is a plausibility check, not a full calibration. Do not vary sprinkler air pressure to create test points.

If an accessible A0 voltage is outside GND to the measured ADC supply, disconnect power and investigate. The ADC gain setting does not make its input tolerant of raw 5V.

End-to-end completion plan
  1. Complete the electrical tests and the live gauge comparison. Retain raw volts, sensor-fault states and uncalibrated labeling until measurements justify changing them.
  2. Before an operational release, harden/test ADC conversion-ready checks, truthful sample age, sensor reconnect behavior, stale readings, and network recovery. A successful compile is not those tests.
  3. Reuse a verified always-on office host for timestamped collection/history. Select and verify the existing host before installing a service; this guide does not create one.
  4. Use an offsite heartbeat receiver to detect loss of the office collector/network/power. The ESP cannot report its own power failure, and a local-only watchdog cannot cover a whole-office outage.
  5. Implement persistent low-temperature, low-pressure, sensor-fault and recovery notifications with hysteresis, deduplication and duration handling. 45F early/under-40F urgent are proposed temperature starting points, not validated fire-protection settings. Pressure thresholds need site-specific validation.
  6. Test actual notification delivery, prolonged conditions, recovery, restart, cable disconnection and power/network outage. Close only after owner operational acceptance.
This device has no outputs to operate valves, the compressor, heating or fire alarms. It remains supplementary and does not replace listed supervision, heating or inspections.
