## Fatal or high-priority errors to correct before any candidate PCB layout

| ID / severity | Origin and actual claim | Independent finding | Required correction / acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| C-01 / **BLOCKER** | Carbon §4.2 proposes TI BQ25606 as a selectable LiFePO4 charger. | TI explicitly rates BQ25606 for **Li-ion/Li-polymer**, with 4.2–4.4 V battery regulation; a nominal 3.2 V LiFePO4 cell requires chemistry-appropriate charge regulation (typically near 3.6–3.65 V, subject to chosen cell). Using BQ25606 as written risks overcharge; it is also a 24-pin VQFN, conflicting with easy hand assembly. | Reject BQ25606 in that role; select a true LiFePO4 charger with NTC/charge inhibit and verify cell-specific charge profile, pack protection, power path, cold test. No onboard battery in initial base unit. [TI BQ25606](https://www.ti.com/product/BQ25606). Carbon's alternative CN3058E **is** described as a LiFePO4 charger; it still needs real design and system power-path validation ([CN3058E datasheet reproduction](https://www.alldatasheet.com/html-pdf/1133208/CONSONANCE/CN3058E/3523/5/CN3058E.html)). |
| C-02 / **BLOCKER** | Carbon §2/§4 claims a single 3.2 V, 3500 mAh LiFePO4 26650 supports **24+ hours** of continuous monitoring while driving ESP32, W5500 and 24 V current loop. | Nameplate energy = **11.2 Wh**. At an illustrative **80% usable end-to-end** energy efficiency this is 8.96 Wh. For 24 hours continuous monitoring, average consumption must be **≤0.373 W**; Claude's plausible wired monitor load target is **1.2–1.5 W**, implying only **~6.0–7.5 h** before additional cold derating. Carbon provides no validated load/time budget supporting 24 h. | Replace 24 h promise with a measured load profile, cold-capacity derating and appropriately sized battery **including powered backhaul**, or omit battery from base. |
| C-03 / **BLOCKER** | Carbon §4.2 uses 150 Ω shunt and ADS1118 at 3.3 V; declares 22 mA = 3.3 V and short-fault threshold above that. | A 150 Ω shunt produces **3.0 V at 20 mA, 3.15 V at 21 mA, 3.30 V at 22 mA, 3.60 V at 24 mA, 3.90 V at 26 mA**. PGA range ±4.096 V does **not** permit pin voltage above ADC VDD. Overrange/fault current will clamp or risk damaging analog inputs unless an adequately engineered front-end limits voltage and current. | Favor **100 Ω** precision shunt (0.4–2.0 V over 4–20 mA, 2.6 V at 26 mA), separate protected terminal-voltage monitoring and appropriately rated clamps. Validate absolute-max and measurement error across every fault. [TI ADS1118](https://www.ti.com/product/ADS1118); [TI ADS1115](https://www.ti.com/product/ADS1115). |
| C-04 / **HIGH** | Carbon §4.3 classifies loop-open at <3.6 mA and loop-short above 21 mA, as if always distinguishable from one shunt reading. | In a field loop an **L+ to L− short can bypass the low-side measurement resistor** or collapse transmitter supply; it need not raise sensed current above 21 mA. Under-/over-range and true shorts also depend on device-specific failure behavior. | Measure protected **loop supply voltage, terminal voltage and shunt current** (optionally high-side current), test open, L+ short-to-ground, L+/L− short, reverse, leakage, and supply undervoltage. Classify unknown rather than false normal. Claude §4.4 offers the stronger provisional classifier. |
| C-05 / **HIGH** | Carbon §4.2 specifies TPS61040 3.3/5 V → 18–24 V up to 35 mA without converter proof. | TI rates TPS61040 for 400 mA **internal switch current** and illustrates substantially lower-load examples; a 24 V × 35 mA = **0.84 W output** from 3.3 V requires ~0.32 A average input at 80% efficiency **before inductor peak and other conversion losses**. It may not meet the claimed combination. | Calculate peak inductor current and efficiency, then simulate and bench-test under warm/cold, low battery and short conditions. Specify a converter with measured margin. [TI TPS61040](https://www.ti.com/product/TPS61040). |
| C-06 / **HIGH** | Carbon §5 projects **$45 industrial pressure transmitter at qty 1, $24 at qty 100**, and $101 fully burdened at 100 units. | No matching supplier quotes are provided. Spot checks show a WIKA A-10 **0–200 psi, 4–20 mA, 1/4 NPT** at **$153.04** retail and a Dwyer **0–100 psi, 4–20 mA, 1/4 NPT** product at **$237.99**; exact configurations and discounts vary. Even WIKA 150 psi G1/4 with a different port is about $145.58. These do **not** prove high-volume pricing, but undermine treating $24 as a known sourceable qualified industrial transmitter price. | Vendor-qualified BOM with **exact range, port, connector, wetted material, proof/burst, accuracy, environmental rating, lead time and tiered quote**. Do not freeze selling prices from Carbon estimates. [WIKA US shop](https://shop.wika.com/en-us/a_10.WIKA?itemId=50398083); [Dwyer seller](https://www.zoro.com/dwyer-instruments-pressure-transducer-0-to-100-psi-626-10-gh-p1-e4-s1/i/G0056341/). |
| C-07 / **HIGH** | Carbon §4.2 says LiFePO4 has “no thermal runaway/fire risk”, and sub-zero discharge is “completely safe”. | That language is an unsupported categorical safety claim; lower-risk chemistry is **not** zero risk, and pack temperature/current limits vary by cell, enclosure and protection circuits. | Design from **actual cell/pack manufacturer datasheet**, use pack protections and cold qualification, seek qualified electrical product review, and avoid marketing zero-risk claims. |
| F-01 / **BLOCKER** | Flash §4.1 selects 0.5–4.5 V signal directly into ADS1115 while depicting a 3.3 V logic ADC supply; also proposes >4.8 V short-detection threshold. | For an ADS1115 powered by 3.3 V, the analog input absolute limit is about **VDD + 0.3 V = 3.6 V**, irrespective of PGA selected. A raw 4.5–4.8 V signal is outside limits; reading it reliably as a fault is not possible without external conditioning. | Use properly tested **divider/clamps** and supply sensing (as the existing *prototype* attempts), or choose appropriate ADC supply/level shifting. A high-PGA range is **not** higher pin tolerance. [TI ADS1115](https://www.ti.com/product/ADS1115). |
| F-02 / **HIGH** | Flash §3.2 says the ADS1115 makes ratiometric pressure output “cancel VDD drift” automatically. | ADS1115 has an **internal** voltage reference; the 0.5–4.5 V sensor changes with its own 5 V excitation unless supply is measured and the result appropriately normalized. | If retaining a voltage sensor, measure sensor excitation with a protected input and calculate the ratio; include excitation and divider tolerances in the error budget. |
| F-03 / **BLOCKER** | Flash §1/§4.2 promises **10–15 s dying-gasp backup** from two series 1 F/2.7 V supercaps (effective 0.5 F) as the supply falls 5.0 → 3.4 V. | Energy = ½×0.5 F×(5²−3.4²) = **3.36 J**. Even at an optimistic **0.5 W** this is only **6.72 seconds ideal**, at 1.0 W **3.36 seconds ideal**, before efficiency/cold/aging. Fifteen seconds requires average draw **≤0.224 W ideal**. Hardwired Ethernet/MCU operation plus conversion losses do not support the claimed backup time. | Remove the hold-time promise, size supercaps from **measured** live-power budget, and verify both upstream network power and ability to transmit; otherwise rely on independent offsite silence alert. |
| F-04 / **BLOCKER** | Flash §4.2 treats **24 VAC** as interchangeable with 9–30 VDC and proposes a **TPS54202** buck and **SMAJ28CA** TVS. | 24 VAC RMS has **33.94 V peak** before bridge losses/line excursions. TPS54202 is rated only **4.5–28 V input**; SMAJ28CA is a **28 V standoff** TVS with breakdown starting **31.1 V**, overlapping nominal 24 VAC peak. This exact proposed combination is not an acceptable unqualified 24 VAC front-end. | For first commercial variant select **low-voltage DC/PoE only**. If AC variant becomes necessary, separately design/qualify rectification, surge, UVLO, dissipation and all maximum voltages (LM5164 can be a different high-voltage candidate). [TI TPS54202](https://www.ti.com/product/TPS54202); [Diodes SMAJ28CA](https://www.diodes.com/part/view/SMAJ28CA). |
| F-05 / **HIGH** | Flash §4.2 calls **MBR140** a “full-wave bridge rectifier”. | MBR140 family is a **single Schottky diode**, not a four-diode bridge device. The drawn AC path is not a complete valid bridge BOM. | Supply a real bridge circuit with four appropriately rated diodes, or a verified integrated bridge with drawings, then rectify/test — only if AC support is required. [onsemi family](https://www.onsemi.com/pdf/datasheet/mbr140esf-d.pdf). |
| F-06 / **HIGH** | Flash §2/§6 uses presumed 20 psi valve-trip hint and ready-made 35/42/45/58 psi color alarm examples. | **Nothing in the competition inputs verifies a universal trip threshold or legal pressure band.** Existing site ~50–54 psi is a measured baseline, not a fire-protection setting. | Keep site thresholds visibly **UNVERIFIED** and do not enable trip-margin claims until qualified contractor reviews the actual valve, air-maintenance device, pressure at approved sensor location and site-specific requirements. |

## Strengths and limitations requiring development rather than rejection

| Item | Judgment / required proof |
|---|---|
| **Claude §4.4 industrial 4–20 mA loop classification** | The strongest proposed fault-diagnostic strategy: compare measured current with terminal voltage and optional high-side current, and require a known-good external reference. **Still only a block-level proposal**, not a tested circuit, especially under open/short/leak/high common-mode and power-off. Validate parts, analog grounding, 24 V injection limits, ADC common-mode and hardware current limits on isolated benchtop equipment. |
| **Claude §4.9 independent backend checks** | Strong operational design: cloud sample-age check + external device heartbeat + separate backend service heartbeat. Need prove alert independence, provider failures, silent credential expiry, clock drift, mobile delivery, acknowledgement escalation, and actual end-to-end fault response. A powered-down device cannot deliver an alert; provider delivery latency must be measured separately from dispatch latency. |
| **Claude §4.5 remote SHT45 on I2C** | Remote I2C over 1–3 m can work with low clock and correct bus capacitance but remains a **field-cable failure and EMI design risk**; this site already has a DS18B20 1-Wire probe and pull-up. For base Rev B, prefer protected remote 1-Wire (CRC, disconnection, −127/85 °C sentinel handling), with optional humidity only if a distinct maintenance case emerges. |
| **Claude extra complexity** | Two analog channels/ADCs, high-side monitor, optional secure element and LTE, multiple humidity sensors and redundant watchdog layers are **not all first-release requirements**. Preserve hardware expansion pads or BOM options; stage only what demonstrably improves measurement trust or availability. |
| **Claude economics** | Transparent material ranges and hands-on labor assumptions are more realistic than cheap single-number bids, but still **not supplier quotes** and do not include tooling, engineering NRE, field installation, warranty, full compliance or product liability in all-in table. Its high sensor cost fits spot retail price checks better. |
| **Carbon §5 production jig and §6 UI** | Useful detailed fixture (current-loop simulation + physical pneumatic verification), clear stale-value states, notification escalation and multi-site data hierarchy. Fix electrical/power errors before incorporating any of its power subsystem into a PCB. |
| **Flash architecture simplicity** | Good proof that modest 2-layer PCB + accessible modules + a well-equipped workshop is viable. Useful inspiration for the EXISTING voltage-sensor prototype; **not** a sufficient electrically protected commercial schematic as proposed. |
| **All: abnormal pressure vs actual trip** | Sensor on the **compressor outlet** may read a different pressure from the actual downstream dry-system gas if separated by regulator, check valve or air-maintenance device. Only a qualified sprinkler contractor can approve tap location and confirm interpretation; the live site measurements are **not** proof of actual valve-side pressure or verified alarm values. Do not infer valve actuation solely from gas-pressure trends. |
| **All: PoE and Wi-Fi failure independence** | A PoE-powered device with a working switch can still fail with the router/switch during a common site outage. Wi-Fi fallback sharing the same site power/internet does not cure that. Either provide externally UPS-backed PoE AND upstream internet or use an independently powered LTE path if monitoring must continue. Independent **cloud-side offline notification** is mandatory regardless. |
| **All: enclosure** | Bought IP65/NEMA-style boxes do not guarantee the **modified assembled product** retains its rating after drilling cable glands, venting, printing mounts and installing the sensor. Validate seal, condensation, temperature, service access and strain relief; do not claim an independently untested finished assembly's certification. |
| **All: freeze risk** | Industry NFSA guidance says the **wet portion of a dry-pipe valve should remain ≥40°F**, and qualifying inspection/low-temp alarms have separate rules. A supplemental, unlisted monitor does not relax mandatory inspection/heating obligations. Do not borrow arbitrary 30/45/60-minute delay examples as accepted emergency policy. [NFSA winter guidance](https://nfsa.org/cold-hard-facts-protecting-sprinklers-from-frost/). |
| **All: OSHA/listing/FCC** | Existing listed electrical supplies and FCC modular radio approvals do **not** confer listing or FCC conformity on the assembled product. OSHA's NRTL program applies to many workplace electrical equipment types. Determine exact equipment classification and installation approval with a qualified electrical/fire-protection review before customer pilots. [OSHA NRTL FAQ](https://www.osha.gov/nationally-recognized-testing-laboratory-program/frequently-asked-questions); [47 CFR §15.212](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/15.212). |
| **All: cloud economics** | At one heartbeat per minute, **100 devices = 144,000 incoming events/day**, exceeding Cloudflare Workers Free's published **100,000/day** limit even before dashboard traffic. Paid Workers, DB writes, cron, storage, SMS numbers, 10DLC and service monitoring need a fresh actual price model. [Cloudflare Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/); [Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/). |

## Physical math and error-budget checkpoints

These calculations are independently recomputed from the proposals' stated figures; they are **not** lab tests.

| Check | Result | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon battery energy | 3.2 V × 3.5 Ah = **11.2 Wh nameplate** | At 80% usable output, 8.96 Wh; only 6.0–7.47 h at 1.5–1.2 W average; 24 h requires total ≤0.373 W. |
| Flash hold-up energy | ½ × 0.5 F × (5.0²−3.4²) = **3.36 J** | At 0.5 W only 6.72 s ideal; at 1.0 W 3.36 s ideal; never promise 15 s without measurement and revised sizing. |
| AC rectification | 24 V RMS × √2 = **33.94 V peak** | Incompatible with a 28 V maximum-input buck or 28 V standoff TVS unless appropriate power design changes. |
| Carbon 150 Ω shunt | 4/12/20/22/26 mA = **0.60/1.80/3.00/3.30/3.90 V** | Too close to or above a 3.3 V ADC rail in fault states; rework shunt and clamp architecture. |
| Proposed 100 Ω shunt | 4/12/20/26 mA = **0.40/1.20/2.00/2.60 V** | Better input headroom for 3.3 V ADC, subject to full protection/current-limit design and noise budget. |
| Cloud heartbeat input | 100 × 60 × 24 = **144,000/day** | Review Workers/D1 plan, alert provider billing and plan headroom; never assume free. |

## Verification checklist — prohibit “fabrication ready” until passed

1. Contractor-confirm valve model, manufacturer air pressure band, normal compressor cut-in/out, alarm/trip margins, process tap location, max pneumatic test pressure and required maintained heating; document approval. Do not touch live trim without authorization.
2. Provide actual sensor **full manufacturer MPNs** with electrical, mechanical, wetted-material, process, temperature, proof and burst ratings, stock/lead time, and second source. The proposed TE 100 psi M3200 family does not automatically meet a 316L wetted-parts requirement: verify actual configuration ([example manufacturer record](https://www.te.com/en/product-20010080-00.html)).
3. Draw real KiCad complete signal/power circuits, test notes and error/power/thermal budgets; verify ADC input absolute limits, sensor loop compliance voltage, short-circuit survival and reference accuracy. ERC/DRC/simulation are **planned**, not already done.
4. Bench fault-injection under isolated, current-limited supply: open, short-to-ground, L+–L− short, swapped wires, 24 V miswire, leakage, noisy/out-of-range sensor, ESD model, power removal, PoE supply, temperature-probe open/short and ADC bus failure. Keep bad values UNKNOWN, not 0 or green.
5. Independent reference-gauge pneumatic test (0/25/50/75/100/150 psi when rated), cable-length and cold/condensation testing. Current-loop simulator alone verifies front-end, **not the transmitter**.
6. End-to-end notification test: cloud heartbeat and dead-man process, backend outage, expired credentials, SMS consent/10DLC, delayed push, contact unreachable, acknowledgement, repeat/escalation, network outage and upstream power failure. Include failures of monitoring provider itself.
7. Commercial gate: approved install and product classifications, FCC/EMC assessment, applicable NRTL safety review, battery shipping if any, legal/insurance/support obligations. Never treat favorable concept scores as permission to install or market.

**Net judgment:** Claude is the best **architecture and validation-process foundation**, **not** a completed reference schematic. Carbon and Flash contain useful features, but their above blockers must be excluded from the selected design.
